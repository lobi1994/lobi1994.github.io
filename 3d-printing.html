Making

3D Printing

From a CAD model to a finished part. My key project is a toy airplane for a child: a 3D puzzle that clicks together with magnets and glows in the dark.

Fusion 360 · Solid Edge · CATIA · FDM printing

The idea

I print functional parts, parts from my own CAD designs, and figures and decor. I currently print on a Creality Ender and am looking at an upgrade.

The toy airplane is my key project so far. It is made of many parts: a 3D puzzle for a child that can be taken apart and built again.

Features

  • Key project: toy airplaneA toy for a child, printed in many parts. Magnets are glued into the parts, so it can be taken apart and put back together as often as you like.
  • Maze cylinderA puzzle box for gifts. You hide a gift inside, and it takes a while to find the way to open it.
  • Glow in the darkThe window and the lower front part are printed in special glow-in-the-dark plastic.
  • Functional partsParts that are useful in everyday life.
  • Own CAD designsParts I model myself from scratch.
  • Figures and decorModels and decor for fun, for example a Poké Ball case for a Switch game.

How it works

01

Design

Fusion 360 for my own parts. At university and at work I used CATIA V5, Solid Edge and AutoCAD.

02

Slice

Bambu Studio, PrusaSlicer or Cura, depending on the job.

03

Print

On a Creality Ender. An upgrade is on my list.

04

Assemble

Multi-part designs like the airplane get magnets glued in, so they can be put together again and again.

Decisions

  • A 3D puzzle in many partsThe airplane is split into parts, so it works as a puzzle for a child.
  • Magnets instead of glueMagnets are glued into the parts, so the airplane can be taken apart and rebuilt.
  • Glow-in-the-dark plasticThe window and the lower front part glow in the dark.
